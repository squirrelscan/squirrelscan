# Diverse DOM classifier: September 19, 2026

The 500-page synthetic-data experiment is complete. The new sparse model improves
on the previous model, but does not beat the semantic HTML-tag baseline for
component type. Frozen RoBERTa is not a clear replacement. These results do not
justify scanner integration yet.

## Corpus and provenance

- 500 fresh pages across 196 source domain groups, selected from 534 preserved
  captures. The seed inventory contains 405 cloud-run website targets.
- Product Hunt data is separate and unchanged. No historical report is treated
  as DOM evidence. All selected URLs are non-root; that sampling fact does not
  prove their semantic page type.
- Jev v4 produced 500 page labels and 3,000 element labels: six elements per page,
  with at least three meaningful non-unknown element labels on every page.
- Independent Luna produced 800 validated blind judgments: each validation/test
  page plus three elements, and 50 training QA pages plus three elements.
  Three invalid batches were rejected and rerun; no heuristic label repair was used.
- Train/validation/test: 350/75/75 pages. Domain and exact-content groups were
  frozen before training. Final redirect destinations do not cross splits.
- Training uses only Jev synthetic probabilities. Human labels were neither
  modified nor used as training targets. Raw pages, labels, credentials and
  models remain in private storage outside this repository.

## Held-out comparison

The fair old-versus-new comparison has 71 pages across 39 domains after excluding
four pages from a domain present in the previous model's training data. Scores
measure agreement with independent **Luna synthetic labels, not human accuracy**.
F1 below is micro F1. Complete examples: component 212, region 150, purpose 138,
page type 71, content kind 69. Partial observations are scored separately as
positive-only recall in the JSON artifact.

| Model | Component accuracy | Region F1 | Purpose F1 | Page-type F1 | Content-kind F1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Previous sparse model | 45.3% | 66.9% | 31.5% | 4.0% | 33.0% |
| New sparse model | 51.9% | 68.5% | 43.8% | 5.6% | 51.5% |
| Frozen RoBERTa + trained heads | 45.8% | 45.5% | 42.4% | 14.8% | 43.0% |
| HTML-tag baseline | 56.1% | 66.1% | 0.0% | 0.0% | 0.0% |
| Jev teacher | 57.5% | 78.0% | 46.7% | 35.4% | 49.8% |

The new sparse model's region macro F1 fell from 28.3% to 18.3%, despite its small
micro-F1 improvement: rare classes remain a weakness. The full JSON includes
per-label support, macro F1, exact-set scores, the complete 75-page test set,
and validation results. Zero-support labels are retained explicitly.

## Interpretation and next work

Forty test component disagreements are “layout container” versus “content
section.” The label boundary needs clearer shared definitions before scaling.
In the parent's 20 blind sanity checks, two empty frames lacked evidence of
purpose and two inputs contained CSS or structured-data text. The remaining
16 supported bounded claims; this small, partial audit is not a gold test set.

The corpus contains substantially more article, commerce, documentation, contact
and listing content than the prior homepage-heavy sample. Jev suggests 96 article
body regions, 17 advertisement regions, 40 buy-box regions, 19 product galleries,
22 product-detail regions and 29 product-review regions. These are provisional
synthetic suggestions. There are only 11 video-player suggestions and no audio
players. Expand rare classes deliberately rather than claiming complete coverage.

Before the next model iteration: clean visible-text extraction, preserve useful
semantic attributes for empty elements, clarify container/content distinctions,
and separate primary page type from secondary traits. Use a small human-reviewed
holdout to settle ambiguous labels before expanding toward 50,000 labels.

## Runtime and validation

The sparse fit took approximately 523 seconds and produced a 133 MB artifact.
The frozen RoBERTa encoder plus trained heads fit took 130.4 seconds;
its heads are 1.1 MB and still require the approximately 499 MB base encoder.
The base model is pinned to `FacebookAI/roberta-base` revision
`e2da8e2f811d1448a5b465c236feacd80ffbac7b` and uses text/DOM only.

Full encoder fine-tuning was attempted but stopped before a completed epoch.
Fixed-shape operations and activation checkpointing reduced its measured memory
footprint from 14.4 GB to 4.2 GB; its projected local runtime remained roughly
45 minutes for three epochs. No full fine-tuning accuracy is claimed. The frozen
encoder alternative was added after inspecting the sparse results and runtime;
this is an exploratory comparison, not an untouched confirmatory model-selection
test. Fitting never reads Luna/test labels. Successful fit durations exclude
failed and stopped attempts.

Validation: 31 focused Python tests pass, all 800 review records join exactly to
frozen inputs, and prediction identities match across every model. Original
captures and rejected responses are preserved. No merge, release, deployment or
private gitlink update was performed. OpenCode crawl processes were untouched.

[Aggregate comparison JSON](comparison-2026-09-19.json) contains hashes, runtime
versions, support counts and metrics. [Reproduction workflow](README.md) links
the acquisition, materialization, training and comparison commands.
