- `security/leaked-secrets` findings carry `meta.context`: a redacted snippet of up to 120 characters either side of the match, so you can find the key in the source without refetching it. The key is masked as in the item id. Nearby quoted literals keep none of their characters, whatever their length. A stray apostrophe (as in a `// don't` comment) or a quote in a regex does not unmask a literal after it when that literal is an assigned value, an argument, a list item, a `||` or `&&` operand, or has no whitespace in it (`echo 'a;b'`, `-p'S3cr;t'`). Unquoted values after `=`, `:` or `=>` are replaced by asterisks up to the next whitespace or quote, whatever their length, so `n=482` and `cvv=91` show none of their characters, and when the key names a secret (`password`, `DB_PASS`, `dbPassword`, `api_key`, `pin`) up to the end of the line, so `password: correct horse battery staple` and `DB_PASS=Tr0ub;4dor&x` show none of their words. A run of 12 or more characters after a secret's label such as `password`, `passcode`, `pin`, `API key` or `X-Api-Key` is starred out, and so are auth-scheme tokens, long token-shaped runs, letter-and-digit runs and URL credentials. Known limits: after a stray quote, a literal that holds whitespace and code punctuation in a place that is not an operand (`echo 'a b;c'`) can show, and so can a word of under 12 letters only after a label with no `=` or `:` (`password hunter`). Code punctuation between two literals can be starred out with them. See [security/leaked-secrets](https://docs.squirrelscan.com/rules/security/leaked-secrets). (squirrelscan/repo#2215)
