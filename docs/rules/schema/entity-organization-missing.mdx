## The node everything else hangs off

An organization node is the centre of a site's graph. It is the publisher of your articles, the owner of your `WebSite`, the seller behind your offers and the subject of your reviews. Without it, each of those is a claim with no subject.

It is also where `sameAs` lives, which is how a search engine connects the company on your site to the company on LinkedIn, Crunchbase and Wikidata. That corroboration is most of what a knowledge panel is assembled from.

The rule only fires when the site presents as a business. A documentation site legitimately has no organization, and telling it to declare one would be advice to publish markup that is not true. A personal site is also left alone, even though it has an about page and a contact page: when the site declares a named `Person` and no products or offers, the thing behind it is that person.

## What squirrel checks

The rule runs once per crawl, reads the [entity map](/entity-map) rather than the pages, and emits a single check named `entity-organization-missing`.

- Skipped with the message `No entity map was built for this audit` and the reason `entity-map-unavailable` when the run produced no map. This is not the same as a site with no structured data, and the rule refuses to conflate them: passing here would assert it had checked something it never looked at.
- Reports at info with the message `This site declares no JSON-LD entities on any crawled page` when the map exists and is empty.
- Passes with the message `This site declares N organization entities`.
- Skipped with the message `This site does not present as a business` and the reason `no-business-signals`.
- Skipped with the message `This site presents a person rather than an organization` and the reason `personal-site`, when a named `Person` is declared and no products or offers are.
- Reports at info with the message `This site presents as a business but declares no Organization or LocalBusiness entity`, with the signals that made it a question listed in the value.

Two signals count as presenting as a business: an `AboutPage` or `ContactPage` entity, and `Product` or `Offer` entities. A site transacting with nothing to say who it is transacting with is the second case.

`Organization`, `Corporation` and the `LocalBusiness` subtypes all satisfy the rule. The subtype list is explicit rather than pattern-matched, because the names do not follow one: `BankOrCreditUnion` and `Dentist` are subtypes and end in neither `Business` nor `Service`, while `OnlineStore` ends in `Store` and is not one.

Findings are capped at 10 items, and the count in the message is always the true total rather than the capped length. Run `squirrel entities -f json` and read the `entities` key for the full list.

## How to fix it

Declare it once, with everything that identifies you:

```json
{
  "@type": "Organization",
  "@id": "https://example.com/#organization",
  "name": "Example Ltd",
  "url": "https://example.com/",
  "logo": "https://example.com/logo.png",
  "sameAs": [
    "https://www.linkedin.com/company/example",
    "https://x.com/example"
  ]
}
```

If you have a physical location customers visit, use a `LocalBusiness` subtype instead and add the address, phone and opening hours. Then reference this `@id` as the publisher of your articles and the owner of your `WebSite`. See [fixing structured data identity](/entity-map/fixing).

## Enable / disable

### Disable this rule

```toml squirrel.toml
[rules]
disable = ["schema/entity-organization-missing"]
```

### Disable all Structured Data rules

```toml squirrel.toml
[rules]
disable = ["schema/*"]
```

### Enable only this rule

```toml squirrel.toml
[rules]
enable = ["schema/entity-organization-missing"]
disable = ["*"]
```
