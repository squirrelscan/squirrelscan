## What rating scope is

A rating in structured data makes two claims: that this rating is about the thing this page is about, and that a reader can see the same rating in the page content. Google's structured data guidelines require both. Markup a reader cannot verify, or markup that rates the site on a page that cannot be rated, is a policy violation rather than a validation error, and the risk is a manual action against the site's rich results rather than a lost snippet on one page.

The usual cause is a template. A review badge added to a shared layout emits the same `AggregateRating` on every page, including the privacy policy, the terms page and every blog post. [schema/review](/rules/schema/review) checks the shape of that markup and passes it, because the shape is fine. This rule asks the two questions the shape check cannot.

## What squirrel checks

The rule walks the page's flattened JSON-LD to a depth of 6. An `aggregateRating` property is attributed to the node that holds it, typed or not, and only the first object in an array-wrapped value is read. A standalone `AggregateRating` node is attributed to the nearest enclosing typed node. A page with no rating emits nothing. Otherwise it emits one or two checks:

- `rating-visible`: passes with the message `AggregateRating backed by a third-party review widget on the page` when the page loads a known review platform such as Trustpilot, Yotpo, Judge.me or Feefo, or carries one of their placeholder containers. It passes with `AggregateRating has a rating visible on the page` when the page's text carries a star glyph, a phrase like `4.9 / 5`, `4.9 out of 5`, `4.9 stars`, `rated 4.9` or `218 reviews`, or the decimal rating value with either a dot or a comma as the separator. The text it searches is the extracted page content plus every `alt`, `aria-label` and `title` attribute. Otherwise it warns with `<rating> on <entity> is not visible anywhere on <page> — a rating readers cannot see is a structured-data policy violation and risks a manual action, not just a lost rich result`, where `<rating>` is `AggregateRating 4.7 from 218 reviews`, `<entity>` is `LocalBusiness "Acme"` and `<page>` is `this privacy policy page`.
- `rating-subject`: emitted only on a page classified as a privacy policy, a terms page or an article. It passes with the message `AggregateRating on this <page kind> page rates the page's own subject` when nothing on the page is out of scope, where `<page kind>` is `privacy policy`, `terms` or `article`. Otherwise it warns with `AggregateRating on <entity> is not the subject of this <page kind> page — sitewide rating markup on a page that cannot be rated is a structured-data policy violation and risks a manual action, not just a lost rich result`.

The rule is deliberately hard to trip. A review widget clears the visibility check outright, because a client-side badge is legitimately absent from crawled HTML. A whole-number rating such as `5` printed in the page text does not clear it on its own, since the digit is too common to be evidence; a decimal such as `4.7` does. On an article page only a carrier that represents the site itself is reported: every LocalBusiness subtype plus `Organization`, `Corporation`, `OnlineBusiness`, `OnlineStore`, `NGO`, `EducationalOrganization`, `GovernmentOrganization`, `PerformingGroup`, `SportsOrganization`, `Brand`, `WebSite` and `WebPage`. An article that reviews a `Product` keeps its rating. On privacy and terms pages every rating is reported, because those pages have no rateable subject at all. Entities listed per check are capped at 10, and the rule is skipped on a page detected as a soft 404.

## How to fix it

```html
<div class="reviews">
  <p>Rated 4.7 out of 5 from 218 reviews</p>
</div>
```

Move the rating markup onto the pages whose subject it describes, which is the `LocalBusiness` on the homepage and location pages, or the `Product` on its own product page, and print the same numbers where a reader can see them.

The common false positive is a rating phrased in wording the visibility patterns do not cover, such as a rating rendered only as a bar chart with no numbers in text. The check errs towards passing, but a design with no readable rating anywhere is worth changing regardless of what the report says.

## Enable / disable

### Disable this rule

```toml squirrel.toml
[rules]
disable = ["schema/rating-scope"]
```

### Disable all Structured Data rules

```toml squirrel.toml
[rules]
disable = ["schema/*"]
```

### Enable only this rule

```toml squirrel.toml
[rules]
enable = ["schema/rating-scope"]
disable = ["*"]
```
