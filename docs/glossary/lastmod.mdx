**Sitemap lastmod** is the optional `<lastmod>` date on a sitemap entry, meant to record when the content at that URL last changed in a meaningful way.

The value uses the W3C Datetime format: a date such as `2026-03-14`, or a full datetime with a time zone such as `2026-03-14T09:12:00+00:00`. Google's 2023 guidance says it uses `lastmod` as a signal for scheduling crawls of URLs it already knows, and that "last modification" means the last significant change: the main text, the structured data or the links, not a sidebar or a footer.

## Why it matters

The date is only useful while it can be trusted. Google's example is a page that changed seven years ago but whose `lastmod` says yesterday: eventually, it says, it stops believing the site's dates. Its sitemap documentation puts the same rule plainly, saying Google uses `lastmod` only when it is consistently and verifiably accurate. The two common ways a site loses that trust are a generator that writes the build time onto every entry, and a sitemap that reads a different date field from the one the page renders.

## How squirrelscan checks it

[crawl/sitemap-lastmod-churn](/rules/crawl/sitemap-lastmod-churn) looks for build-time stamping. It reads every `<lastmod>` in every discovered sitemap, turns each into a calendar day, and warns when 20 or more dated URLs collapse onto two distinct days or fewer. Google News sitemaps are left out, because they hold about 48 hours of articles by design, and values that name only a year or a month are left out of the sample too.

[crawl/sitemap-lastmod-drift](/rules/crawl/sitemap-lastmod-drift) compares each URL's `lastmod` with the page's own date: the `dateModified` in its JSON-LD first, then other `dateModified` markup, then the visible published date. It warns in two directions, with separate thresholds: `lastmod` behind the page by more than 7 whole days, and ahead by more than 30. Partial days are dropped, so a 7.5-day gap does not warn. Both are rule options. [crawl/sitemap-valid](/rules/crawl/sitemap-valid) separately reports values that are not valid W3C Datetime.

## How to fix it

Write the content's own modification time, and leave it alone when a deploy does not change the page:

```xml
<url>
  <loc>https://example.com/blog/post</loc>
  <lastmod>2026-03-14T09:12:00Z</lastmod>
</url>
```

Take the value from the CMS field or from version control, never from the clock at build time ([the churn fix](/rules/crawl/sitemap-lastmod-churn#how-to-fix-it)). Make the sitemap and the page read the same field in the same order, since `publishedAt ?? updatedAt` on one side and `updatedAt ?? publishedAt` on the other disagree on every edited post ([the drift fix](/rules/crawl/sitemap-lastmod-drift#how-to-fix-it)). Leaving `lastmod` out is better than writing a wrong one.
