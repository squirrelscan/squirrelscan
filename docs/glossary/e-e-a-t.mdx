**E-E-A-T** stands for experience, expertise, authoritativeness and trustworthiness: the qualities Google's search quality raters are asked to look for in content, with trust at the centre.

Google added the first E, for experience, in December 2022, to cover content written from first-hand use of a product or a visit to a place. Its guide to helpful content says that of the four, trust is the most important, that the others contribute to it, and that content does not have to show all of them.

**E-E-A-T is not a score and not a ranking factor.** Google says so directly: "While E-E-A-T itself isn't a specific ranking factor", its systems use a mix of factors that can identify content with good E-E-A-T. The raters do not set rankings either. Google's overview of the rater guidelines says no single rating, and no single rater, directly affects how a page ranks. Their ratings are used in aggregate to check whether the ranking systems work.

## Why it matters

The idea is a checklist for a reader's question: why should I believe this page? Google says its systems give even more weight to content with strong E-E-A-T on topics that could affect people's health, finances or safety, which it calls [YMYL](/glossary/ymyl). A page earns trust through things a reader can check, such as who wrote it, what they know, where the claims come from and who runs the site.

## How squirrelscan checks it

squirrelscan does not measure E-E-A-T and gives no E-E-A-T score. Its rules detect on-page signals that a reader, or a rater, would look for. [eeat/author-byline](/rules/eeat/author-byline) measures the share of content pages with an attributed author, and [eeat/author-expertise](/rules/eeat/author-expertise) looks for author pages or `Person` markup without reading the bios. [eeat/about-page](/rules/eeat/about-page) and [eeat/contact-page](/rules/eeat/contact-page) look for those pages and their contact methods. [eeat/citations](/rules/eeat/citations) counts links to a short, US-centric list of authoritative domains. [eeat/trust-signals](/rules/eeat/trust-signals) and [eeat/editorial-policy](/rules/eeat/editorial-policy) look for review markup, testimonial pages and policy pages by URL. [eeat/authority-signals](/rules/eeat/authority-signals) is a cloud check that warns when a page has neither an author nor citations.

When the audit is confident of the site type, several of these run only on blog, news, health, education or nonprofit sites. Most report at info level.

## How to fix it

Show the reader who is behind the content and what it rests on:

```html
<p class="byline">
  By <a rel="author" href="/authors/jane-diaz">Jane Diaz</a>, Registered Dietitian
</p>
```

Link each byline to an author page that says what the person knows and how ([the byline fix](/rules/eeat/author-byline#how-to-fix-it), [the expertise fix](/rules/eeat/author-expertise#how-to-fix-it)). Cite sources in the sentence that makes the claim ([the citations fix](/rules/eeat/citations#how-to-fix-it)). Write an About page for someone who has never heard of you, and publish real contact details ([the About page fix](/rules/eeat/about-page#how-to-fix-it), [the contact fix](/rules/eeat/contact-page#how-to-fix-it)). It only helps if it is true.

## Related terms

- [YMYL](/glossary/ymyl): Topics that could significantly affect a person's health, finances, safety or society, which Google's raters hold to a higher standard.
- [Author byline](/glossary#author-byline): The visible author name on an article, ideally linked to an author page and matched by an author entity in structured data.
- [Content freshness](/glossary/content-freshness): How current a page is and how consistently it says so: visible dates, dateModified, sitemap lastmod and copyright year.
