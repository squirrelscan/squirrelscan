A **URL slug** is the readable last part of a URL's path, such as `merino-crew-socks` in `https://example.com/products/merino-crew-socks`, usually built from the page title.

Google's URL structure guidance recommends readable words over long ID numbers, and hyphens over underscores to separate words, because hyphens clearly mark a word boundary. It also notes that URLs are case-sensitive: `/APPLE` and `/apple` are different URLs. Characters outside the URL-safe set have to be percent-encoded, so a space becomes `%20`.

## Why it matters

A slug is part of what a searcher sees in the result and what a person reads in a shared link. `/products/merino-crew-socks` says what the page is; `/p/48213` does not. The bigger risks come from inconsistency. Mixed case or a mix of hyphens and underscores creates near-duplicate URLs for one page, and spaces and special characters turn into percent-encoded strings that are hard to read and easy to break. Renaming a live URL has a cost too. It needs a permanent redirect, and the change usually brings a period of ranking movement, so the convention matters most for new pages.

## How squirrelscan checks it

Most URL rules look at one path and report at info or warning level. [url/slug-keywords](/rules/url/slug-keywords) warns when the last segment is a numeric ID, a hash or a generic pattern such as `post-123`, except pagination such as `/page/2`. [url/hyphens](/rules/url/hyphens) warns on underscores in the path, [url/lowercase](/rules/url/lowercase) on uppercase letters, and [url/special-chars](/rules/url/special-chars) on spaces, symbols such as `&` or `#`, non-ASCII characters and double slashes. [url/stop-words](/rules/url/stop-words) notes paths with more than two English stop words, and [url/length](/rules/url/length) notes URLs over 75 characters, warns over 100 and flags more than four path segments. [url/slug-convention](/rules/url/slug-convention) is site-wide. It learns the site's own separator, case and trailing slash, plus date use and depth per page type, and reports the pages that break a convention 70% of comparable pages follow.

Only [url/length](/rules/url/length) looks at the query string, as part of the full URL.

## How to fix it

Generate the slug once, from the title, when the page is created:

```
https://example.com/products/merino-crew-socks
https://example.com/products/merino-crew-socks-48213
```

The second form keeps an ID for routing and still reads correctly ([the slug fix](/rules/url/slug-keywords#how-to-fix-it)). Transliterate to ASCII, lowercase, replace everything else with a hyphen and collapse repeats ([the special character fix](/rules/url/special-chars#how-to-fix-it)). For existing URLs, change the slug at the source and 301-redirect the old path, or leave it until you restructure anyway ([the hyphen fix](/rules/url/hyphens#how-to-fix-it), [the length fix](/rules/url/length#how-to-fix-it)). A site written in a non-Latin script has good reason to keep its characters, and can disable the special character rule.

## Related terms

- [URL parameters](/glossary#url-parameters): Key-value pairs after ? in a URL; tracking and sort parameters can multiply URLs for the same content.
