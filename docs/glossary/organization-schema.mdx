**Organization schema** is schema.org `Organization` structured data that names the business or institution behind a site, with properties such as its `url`, `logo`, contact points and `sameAs` links to its profiles elsewhere.

Google's Organization documentation lists no required properties. It recommends adding the ones that apply, and says the markup can help Google understand the organization's administrative details and disambiguate it in search results, including the logo it shows. `sameAs` lists URLs of pages about the same organization, such as its LinkedIn or Wikidata entry. A business with premises customers visit uses a `LocalBusiness` subtype instead, which is still an organization.

## Why it matters

The organization is the publisher of every article and the owner of the site, so other markup points at it. That works best when it is declared once, with a stable `@id`, and referenced everywhere else. The common failures are the opposite: no organization at all on a site that sells things, a full copy of the block on every page with slightly different logos, or two plugins each declaring their own version. `sameAs` is the corroboration, the links that say this is the same company that has these profiles.

## How squirrelscan checks it

[schema/organization](/rules/schema/organization) reads each page's `Organization` or `Corporation` node and warns when `name` or `url` is missing. It notes a missing `logo` and an empty `sameAs` at info level. When the audit knows the site's official social accounts, it warns about any that `sameAs` leaves out. Two rules read the site-wide [entity](/glossary/entity) map. [schema/entity-organization-missing](/rules/schema/entity-organization-missing) notes a site whose markup presents it as a business, through `AboutPage`, `ContactPage`, `Product` or `Offer` entities, but declares no organization. [schema/entity-sameas-missing](/rules/schema/entity-sameas-missing) notes a primary organization with no `sameAs`. [social/social-profiles](/rules/social/social-profiles) looks for links to seven social platforms in each page's markup, and checks known official accounts against them.

## How to fix it

Declare it once, with an absolute `@id`, and reference it elsewhere:

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Organization",
  "@id": "https://example.com/#organization",
  "name": "Acme Coffee",
  "url": "https://example.com/",
  "logo": "https://example.com/logo.png",
  "sameAs": [
    "https://www.linkedin.com/company/acme-coffee",
    "https://github.com/acme-coffee"
  ]
}
```

Put the full block on the homepage ([the organization fix](/rules/schema/organization#how-to-fix-it)). On other pages, refer to it as `{ "@id": "https://example.com/#organization" }`, for example as an article's `publisher` ([the missing organization fix](/rules/schema/entity-organization-missing#how-to-fix-it)). List every profile that is genuinely about this organization in `sameAs` ([the sameAs fix](/rules/schema/entity-sameas-missing#how-to-fix-it)), and link the same accounts from the footer ([the social profiles fix](/rules/social/social-profiles#how-to-fix-it)).
