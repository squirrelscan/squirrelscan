**Product schema** is schema.org `Product` structured data that describes an item for sale or under review, usually with an `Offer` for its price and availability and, where there are any, its reviews and rating.

Google splits product markup into two uses. Product snippets are for pages where people cannot buy directly, such as an editorial review. Merchant listings are for pages where they can. For product snippets, Google requires a `name` and at least one of `review`, `aggregateRating` or `offers`, and its Rich Results Test may warn about `offers` with no review or rating. Merchant listings have their own, longer requirements. Prices, availability and ratings must match what the page shows.

## Why it matters

Product markup is what lets a result show a price, stock status or star rating, and it feeds Google's shopping features. It also drifts. A price changes on the page and not in the JSON-LD, a sale ends but the markup still says `InStock`, or a review widget loads its numbers after the page and leaves the markup with nothing visible to match. Google's structured data guidelines treat markup that misleads or describes hidden content as a policy problem, which can cost the page its rich result eligibility.

## How squirrelscan checks it

[schema/product](/rules/schema/product) finds the page's `Product` node, including one inside an `@graph`, and warns when `name` or `image` is missing. That is squirrelscan's own required set, not Google's. It notes at info level a product with no `offers`, and warns when the first offer lacks a price or availability. Each test is for a truthy value, so a price of `0` reads as missing. [schema/review](/rules/schema/review) checks that an `AggregateRating` has a `ratingValue` and a `ratingCount` or `reviewCount`, and that each `Review` has an author and a rating. [schema/rating-scope](/rules/schema/rating-scope) warns when the rating is not visible on the page. A known review widget such as Trustpilot or Yotpo on the page counts as visible.

## How to fix it

Generate the markup from the same data that renders the page:

```html
<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Product",
  "name": "Ceramic pour-over dripper",
  "image": "https://example.com/dripper.jpg",
  "offers": {
    "@type": "Offer",
    "price": "32.00",
    "priceCurrency": "USD",
    "availability": "https://schema.org/InStock"
  }
}
</script>
```

Keep price and availability in step with what a shopper sees ([the product fix](/rules/schema/product#how-to-fix-it)). Put the rating on the product it rates and print the same numbers on the page ([the review fix](/rules/schema/review#how-to-fix-it), [the rating scope fix](/rules/schema/rating-scope#how-to-fix-it)). Validate the result in Google's Rich Results Test.
